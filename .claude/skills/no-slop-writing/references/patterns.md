# Pattern catalog

Each pattern: what to watch for, why it fails, how to fix it. Strength markers:
**[1x]** = act on a single sighting, **[cluster]** = act only when it repeats or stacks with
other tells in the same passage.

The fix is always the same move: go back to the fact and write the plainest true sentence.
Do not replace a flagged phrase with a synonym.

---

## A. Staging instead of stating

The sentence signals importance instead of adding information. Strongest tells in current
model output.

### A1. Contrast reveal [1x]
**Watch for:** "It's not X, it's Y" · "Not just X, but Y" · "X isn't the problem. Y is." ·
"More than a X" · "stops being X and starts being Y" · the split form ("This doesn't mean X.
It means Y.") · a clipped tail ("..., no guessing").
**Why:** the negative half names something nobody claimed, so the positive half sounds bigger.
Weight without information.
**Fix:** state Y. Keep a contrast only when the reader really believes X, or when both halves
carry a fact.
> Before: Not just a to-do list. A second brain for your team.
> After: Shared task lists with comments and due dates.

### A2. Negation list [1x]
**Watch for:** "No setup. No credit card. No headaches." · "Not a X. Not a Y. A Z."
**Fix:** say what does happen. Keep one absence if it matters to the decision ("No card
required").

### A3. Dramatic fragments and one-line closers [1x]
**Watch for:** "Fast. Private. Yours." · a one-line paragraph restating the paragraph above ·
"That's the real win." · "Simple as that." · "Read that again." · the same closer after every
section · ALL CAPS or "every. single. day."
**Why:** asks the reader to pause on a claim instead of adding to it.
**Fix:** merge fragments into one sentence with a specific claim. Cut closers that repeat. Keep a
short line only when it adds a new fact.

### A4. Self-answered question and colon reveal [1x]
**Watch for:** "The result? 3x faster." · "The best part: it learns." · "Why does this matter?
Because..."
**Fix:** delete the question or the reveal, keep the answer as a normal sentence.

### A5. Staged run-up [1x]
**Watch for:** "Here's the thing" · "Here's what you need to know" · "Let's dive in" · "Let's
break it down" · "The truth is" · "It turns out" · "Honestly?" · "Look," · "Real talk" ·
"Introducing..." when it adds nothing.
**Fix:** delete the run-up and start with the point.

### A6. Sayings that sound deep [1x]
**Watch for:** "At its core" · "What really matters is" · "X is the language of Y" · "X is not
a tool, it's a mirror" · "the heart of" · aphorisms · anything that sounds like a pull-quote.
**Fix:** replace with the specific claim. If it would look good on a poster, rewrite it.

### A7. Arguing with no one [1x]
**Watch for:** "To be clear" · "Don't get me wrong" · "This isn't about X" · "You might think...
but" · rejecting an option nobody proposed.
**Fix:** remove the defense; keep the claim.

### A8. False agency and abstract subjects [cluster]
**Watch for:** "your workflow, reimagined" · "the data tells a story" · "collaboration comes
alive" · "a complaint becomes a fix" · "the future of work is here".
**Fix:** name who does what. Usually "you" or the product: "You see every comment in one thread."

## B. Inflation

The underlying fact is usually fine. Keep it, drop the dressing.

### B1. Inflated significance [1x]
**Watch for:** "a new era" · "game-changer" · "pivotal" · "plays a key role" · "transform the way
you" · "take X to the next level" · "unlock the power/potential" · "supercharge" · "the future
looks bright" · "revolutionary".
**Fix:** say what changed, with a number or a concrete before/after. Cut send-off sentences.

### B2. Sales adjectives without facts [cluster]
**Watch for:** seamless, effortless, powerful, robust, intuitive, cutting-edge, innovative,
world-class, stunning, beautiful, best-in-class, blazing fast, magic(al), next-gen.
**Fix:** replace each with the fact it stands for: "seamless" → the number of setup steps;
"fast" → the time; "robust" → the scale or failure case handled. Two in one paragraph → rewrite
the paragraph.

### B3. AI-favored vocabulary [cluster]
**Watch for:** delve, leverage, harness, elevate, empower, streamline, unleash, showcase,
underscore, foster, tapestry, landscape, testament, meticulous, intricate, pivotal, crucial,
vibrant, embark, navigate (figurative), realm, journey (for a product flow), ever-evolving.
**Fix:** use the word you would say out loud: use, show, help, start, look at.
**Do not chase:** however, comprehensive, robust (technical sense), "when it comes to" alone.
Readers rarely notice those; over-flagging makes the tool noisy.

### B4. Copula avoidance [cluster]
**Watch for:** serves as, stands as, acts as, boasts, features, offers, represents, functions as.
**Fix:** is, are, has.

### B5. Shallow -ing riders [cluster]
**Watch for:** a fact followed by ", ensuring...", ", empowering...", ", making it easy to...",
", so nothing slips through the cracks".
**Fix:** end the sentence at the claim. Give a real benefit its own sentence.

### B6. Vague authority and proof [1x]
**Watch for:** "Trusted by thousands" · "Loved by teams everywhere" · "Experts agree" ·
"Industry-leading".
**Fix:** a real number, a named customer, a real quote, or cut it. Never invent one.

### B7. Vague declaratives [1x]
**Watch for:** "The implications are significant" · "The stakes are high" · "It makes all the
difference".
**Fix:** name the specific implication, or cut.

## C. Rhythm by rule

### C1. Forced triads [cluster]
Ideas grouped in threes whether or not there are three: "Plan, track, and ship." "Faster,
simpler, smarter." Keep three when there are three real items; otherwise use two, or one, or
four.

### C2. Uniform sentences and paragraph shapes [cluster]
Every sentence the same length; every card the same template (title + two-clause sentence +
benefit); every section ending on a short line. Vary length. Let structure follow content.

### C3. Dashes as the universal connector [cluster]
Em dashes and spaced hyphens joining every clause. In short copy (headlines, buttons, toasts,
subject lines) use none. In long copy, keep them rare. Replace with a period, comma, colon or
parentheses, not an ellipsis.

### C4. Stacked hedges [cluster]
"could potentially", "may help to", "in some cases it might". Keep one qualifier only when the
uncertainty is real.

### C5. Synonym cycling [cluster]
The same thing called project, workspace, space and board in one flow. Pick one term and keep it
everywhere.

### C6. Adverbs and intensifiers [cluster]
really, truly, simply, just, actually, incredibly, effortlessly, seamlessly. Cut. Keep "actually"
only when it corrects something.

### C7. Passive voice hiding the actor [cluster]
"Your changes have been saved" is fine in UI. "Mistakes were made" is not. Use active voice when
it makes the actor and the action clearer.

## D. Formatting by rule

### D1. Bold as decoration [cluster]
Bold on random phrases; lists where every item starts with **Label:**. Remove the bold; turn
labeled lists into plain items or prose when the labels carry nothing.

### D2. Decorative headings and emoji [cluster]
Title Case Every Word, emoji as bullets or section markers, arrows as decoration, a heading that
is written for effect ("The decision, on one screen") instead of naming its content. Use the
project's casing convention (sentence case by default), plain markers, descriptive headings.

### D3. Heading repeated in the first sentence [1x]
"## Performance" followed by "Speed matters." Delete the restatement.

## E. Leftovers

Remove outright.

### E1. Chatbot residue [1x]
"Great question!", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if...", "Certainly!", "Would you like
me to...", "Here is a...".

### E2. Developer notes in the UI [1x]
TODO, FIXME, placeholder, lorem, "replace this", "for now", "temporary", "this section will
display...", instructions addressed to the implementer.

### E3. Leaked internals [1x]
Endpoint paths, table or column names, provider names (Supabase, Vercel...) where the user does
not need them, raw error codes, stack traces, "Error 500", "PGRST116", token, webhook, cache,
build. Translate into what the user experiences: "We couldn't save your changes. Try again in a
moment."

### E4. Writing about the document [1x]
"In this section, we'll...", "Below you'll find...", "This page is organized by...", "We built
this to...".

### E5. Stale or mixed descriptions [1x, needs code]
Copy describing an old flow, a removed option, a different limit, or blending old and new
behavior. Check the component; align the text to the code; ask when intent is unclear.

## F. Wrong reader

### F1. Re-explaining what the reader knows [1x]
Background rebuilt before the point; the action or decision in the last line. Lead with it.

### F2. Restating the visible [1x]
Helper text that repeats the label; instructions that describe the button next to them; an empty
state that says "There's nothing here" with no next step.

### F3. Several ideas in one element [1x]
A button with an explanation inside it; a label that is a full sentence; a toast that confirms
and also upsells. One idea per element.

---

## When not to act

- Quoted text, testimonials, legal text, brand names, code, identifiers.
- A pattern used deliberately and once (one contrast that corrects a real misconception, a punchy
  H1, three real features).
- Ordinary connectives ("however", "also") on their own.
- Text in a voice guide that explicitly calls for a style (match the guide).
